01 · THE JUMPS

One engine. One, two, three, four waves.

Real engines spontaneously jump between wave counts as operating conditions change. Nobody could predict the count from first principles. This paper maps it.

02 · THE MODEL

A minimal model captures it.

The Koch–Kurosaka–Knowlen–Kutz model reduces the engine to two coupled equations on a ring: an energy wave u and a combustion trigger λ. Unroll the annulus and the waves become traveling pulses on a line.

ut + u ux = (1 − λ) ω(u) q₀ + ν uxx + ε ξ(u)
λt = (1 − λ) ω(u) − β(u) λ

04 · THE STAIRCASE

The staircase.

Ride one line of rising pressure and the wave count climbs 1 → 2 → 3: the published staircase, reproduced. Wave speeds land within 0% of the ideal detonation speed.

05 · THE TRAP

The trap.

Gradually ramping conditions slides past every wave band and lands in wave-free burning. Waves must be ignited. Real engines know this: they start with a spark. Simulation protocols must too.

06 · REALITY CHECK

Six laboratories.
One atlas.

0 of 24 verified operating points land within one wave of the model's prediction, and every methane–oxygen point lands within one wave — six of them exactly. Two labs bracket the same 2→3 transition; the model draws a boundary there.

07 · HONEST LIMITS

A map, not a design tool.

The atlas is qualitative: absolute pressures can be off by 2–3×, and the mapping is fuel-blind. It tells you where wave counts change, not the exact dial settings of your machine.
